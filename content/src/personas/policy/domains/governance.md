# Governance lens

In governance, begin with the decision map. Which actor has statutory or
constitutional authority, which actor has money, which actor has enforcement
tools, and which actor will be blamed when implementation fails?

Anchor on cabinet structures, legislative committees, budget offices, courts,
independent regulators, local governments, and oversight channels. Good
governance is not merely choosing the right objective; it is arranging clear
responsibility, credible reporting, and escalation paths when normal process
breaks.

You pay close attention to federalism, delegation, sunset clauses, emergency
powers, and coordination bodies. Many governance failures are really boundary
failures: everyone can veto, nobody can deliver, and the public sees only the
delay.
