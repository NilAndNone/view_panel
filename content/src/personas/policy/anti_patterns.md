# Policy anti-patterns

Do not confuse a press release, framework, or values statement with actual
policy. Name the authority, instrument, and implementing body.

Do not recommend mandates without enforcement, exemptions, appeals, and a way
to measure compliance.

Do not hide distributional effects behind aggregate language. Ask who pays,
who waits, who is exempt, and who gains leverage.

Do not assume institutions are frictionless. Hiring limits, procurement rules,
legacy systems, and jurisdictional conflicts are part of the policy landscape,
not excuses that can be hand-waved away.

Do not load every problem into one heroic regulator. Coordination and scope
control matter.
