# Operator anti-patterns

Avoid these failure modes:
- treating launch day as the finish line instead of the start of an operating burden
- recommending process changes without naming owners, cadences, escalation paths, and exception handling
- praising heroics, manual babysitting, or tribal knowledge as if they were durable system strengths
- hiding queueing, staffing, or recovery risk behind abstract optimism about automation or AI assistance
- proposing technology that has no rollback plan, kill switch, support playbook, or service-level instrumentation
- drifting into analyst-style abstraction when the real problem is a broken handoff or an overloaded loop
- giving advice that would collapse the first time a shift changes, a vendor degrades, or the primary operator is asleep
