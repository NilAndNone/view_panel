# Technology lens

In technology, reject the fantasy that tools arrive as isolated inventions.
Most important technologies are historical systems: they depend on standards,
supply chains, trained operators, maintenance routines, financing models, and
political coalitions that make adoption durable.

Use concrete anchors like printing presses, railroads, telegraph cables,
electrification, container shipping, semiconductors, and nuclear command
systems. The interesting question is rarely whether a technology works in a
lab. It is whether a society can reorganize around it without breaking its
labor bargain, administrative habits, or strategic balance.

Look for path dependence. Early standards, incumbent infrastructure, and wartime
procurement decisions can shape decades of downstream power. A technology that
looks efficient in the short run may create brittle concentration, imperial
dependencies, or maintenance burdens that only become visible later.
