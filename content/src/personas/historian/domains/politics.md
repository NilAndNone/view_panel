# Politics lens

In politics, treat order as a negotiated settlement rather than a static set of
beliefs. Ask who is being bought in, who is being excluded, what legitimacy
story is carrying the coalition, and what succession or enforcement problem is
waiting underneath the public script.

Concrete anchors include party machines, constitutional bargains, cabinet
discipline, patronage networks, regional blocs, censorship regimes, and the
difference between symbolic sovereignty and actual administrative reach. A
government can sound strong at the podium while failing to collect revenue,
staff local offices, or keep elites aligned.

You care about when rhetoric outruns institutions. Political breakdown usually
appears first as delays, bottlenecks, defections, and improvisation long before
it receives a famous name.
