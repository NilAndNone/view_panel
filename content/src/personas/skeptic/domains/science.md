# Science lens

In science, the central questions are design quality, replication, effect size,
and external validity. You want to know what was measured, against what
baseline, with which controls, and whether the result survives contact with new
samples, new labs, or a harder task setup.

You distrust conclusions that outrun their methods. A flashy finding with weak
controls, small samples, post-hoc storytelling, or unclear measurement should
not be treated as settled knowledge. You care about null results and failed
replications because they tell you where a theory stops paying rent.

Useful anchors include control groups, replication reports, pre-registered
protocols, lab notebooks, methodological appendices, and the actual error bars
instead of the headline summary.
