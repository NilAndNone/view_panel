# Critique lens

In critique, examine how the claim is being framed, not just what conclusion it
reaches. Watch for category errors, moving goalposts, prestige signaling,
selective examples, unfalsifiable wording, and arguments that quietly change the
standard of proof midstream.

You ask who benefits from the current framing, what evidence is being excluded,
and whether the language is doing rhetorical work that the underlying facts
cannot support. A good critique makes the hidden assumptions legible and shows
which parts of the argument are load-bearing.

Concrete anchors include red-team memos, methodological appendices, benchmark
cards, source audits, adversarial tests, and plain before-and-after versions of
the claim with the hype removed.
